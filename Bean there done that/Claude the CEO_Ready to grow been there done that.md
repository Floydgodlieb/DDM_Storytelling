# CEO review: "Ready to grow next year?" presentation

Oct 15, 2026 · @Baran

## Verdict

*Speaking as CEO of Bean there done that.*

Yes, I could follow it to the end, and I liked it. You gave me the answer on slide 2 ("not yet, but within a year"), then walked me along my own chain in a logical order: sales, roasting, warehouse, machines, quality, beans, delivery, growth, plan, ask.

Every slide title is a sentence I can repeat to my board. The charts use one accent colour and start at zero. On the last slide you said plainly that €3.6M is sales, not profit, and that you did not guess the cost of a second shift. That honesty makes me trust you more, not less.

Before this meeting I checked several of your numbers against our own file, and they hold:

- Sales are €18.0M, with B2B at 2.3%.
- 30,725 orders over €55 still paid €4.95 shipping.
- We bought 98 containers.
- Machine time is 8.0, 8.5 and 8.3 hours a day.
- 48 tonnes of coffee were scrapped, and light roast is rejected 5% of the time.

But I would not sign the whole plan today. Here is why, as questions.

## Where I lost confidence

**1. The packing line disappears from your growth story.** Slide 2 says packing is full at 8.3 hours a day. The +16% quick win and the growth simulation only look at the roasters. If we fill batches to 120 kg, packing time per batch grows with them, so packing would need about 9.6 hours a day. Doesn't packing become the bottleneck the moment we apply your quick wins? And doesn't your "weeks of second shift" estimate then undercount?

**2. You checked beans, but not bags.** Our webshop sold about 995,000 half-kilo bags last year. Yet the packaging stock only went down by about 170,000 half-kilo bags (100k start, plus 100k bought, minus 10k damaged, minus 20k left in December). The 1 kg bags show the same gap: about 230,000 sold against about 60,000 used from stock. Either that data is wrong, or we buy packaging somewhere we don't record. If the data is right, 20,000 half-kilo bags in December is about one week of sales. That is a bigger risk than Uganda beans. Which one is it?

**3. The timing and the money don't match.** You say: open for new customers in month 9. You also say: €3.6M extra sales per year. If we open in September, next year we get at most four months of growth. Those months are our busiest, together about €6.9M of sales, so +20% means roughly €1.4M, and only if new customers arrive on day one. The "What you get" slide also puts a saving (€0.5M) next to sales (€3.6M) and leaves out what your plan costs me:

- the €152k of shipping fees we will no longer charge,
- the refunds you propose,
- the second shift.

What is the net effect on profit next year?

**4. Which coffee did we throw away?** Your slide says 48 t. Per product, 30 t of it is dark roast: we roasted about 355 t of dark and sold about 321 t. So "roast what we sell" is mainly a dark roast problem. I also don't believe "0 tonnes thrown away" in practice, because we will always need some safety stock. What is a realistic target, and what shelf life do you assume?

**5. Why only the "chase" option?** You propose roasting what we sell each month, plus a second shift in busy weeks. The alternative is to build up some stock in July and August, within shelf life, ahead of the September peak. That could replace part of the second shift. Did you compare the two? I need the cost and staffing of both. Can I even find people for a few separate weeks?

**6. Are the machines really "8 hours full"?** Your machine time is setup plus roasting. If I add cooling time, the roasters are busy 11.6 and 12.6 hours a day. Does cooling block the next batch or not? If it does, we are already far beyond one shift today. And if one shift is 8 hours including breaks and cleaning, how are we running 8.5 hours of pure roasting now?

**7. Are the quick wins realistic?** The small roaster has a 4-minute setup, but it only roasts medium. The big roaster switches between light and dark every day; setup there is 6.3 minutes for dark and 5.7 for light. You blame the same switching for the light roast quality problem. So isn't the real fix to roast light roast in blocks, rather than "setup as fast as the small one"? Also, max batch size is not the same as best batch size. Will a full 120 kg drum hurt quality, especially for light roast?

**8. What does your bean safety level really measure?** You calculate "days of beans" as stock divided by average purchases, not by what we actually use. You also don't know which beans go into which roast. The 10-day safe level is your own assumption, and a container from Uganda is weeks at sea. What matters is whether the next container is already on its way. Shouldn't the safety level depend on lead time?

**9. Delivery: is it really only PostNL?** On my own quick count, on-time depends a lot on how you treat weekends and holidays: I got about 91%, not 94%. Late parcels are indeed spread evenly over the country. But December is about twice as bad as other months, the same month we are short of coffee and PostNL is at peak. Are you sure it's only a PostNL issue?

**10. Is Noord- and Zuid-Holland real growth or just a gap on a chart?** "Orders per 1,000 residents" is a good idea, but it's a hypothesis, not a forecast. Amsterdam and Rotterdam may simply have more competing roasters. How would we win those customers, and what would that cost? Our 21 office clients all buy medium roast in 1 kg bags, and each one ordered exactly 13 times. That demand is steady and easy to plan, which is exactly what my roastery needs. Why isn't B2B a bigger part of your growth advice?

**11. Small things a board member would catch:**

- A week of second shift is "≈18 t" on slide 11 but "≈15 t" in the appendix.
- "1 in 8 customers" should be 1 in 8 orders. Among orders above €55 it's actually 1 in 5.
- "Exactly 658 orders every day, including Christmas" is not normal behaviour. Present it as something odd in the data, not as proof that our stop on new customers caused it.

## My decision

I approve two parts of the fix plan now:

- **The webshop shipping fix.** This is not a saving: it costs us €152k a year. But we promised free shipping above €55, and breaking that promise is a trust risk and possibly a legal one.
- **A monthly roasting plan per product**, starting with dark roast.

Before I commit to growth or a second shift, come back with three things:

1. The packing line capacity, including what the quick wins do to it.
2. An explanation of the packaging stock data.
3. A simple profit view of next year, with costs counted as carefully as gains.

## Coaching note (out of role)

This is a strong presentation: the story structure and visual discipline are clearly above average. Its weak spot is typical for good analyses. Once you found a convincing story, you stopped checking the parts of the chain that didn't fit it: packing, packaging stock and the timing of growth.

Build one habit before presenting. For every recommendation, ask "what is the next bottleneck after this one?" and "does my 'what you get' slide count costs as carefully as gains?" Fixing questions 1 to 3 would make this presentation much harder to argue with.
